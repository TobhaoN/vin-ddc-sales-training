Pipeline &preparation

You can't close what you don't build. Pipeline volume and pre-call data prep are the single biggest difference between top and average territories.

Module 1 · Pipeline & prep

The four habits that fill the funnel

Top reps don't run on hope. They run on a system: more conversations, better data, sharper value, deeper prep.

Habit 1

Conversations create pipeline

Top reps are in front of dealers constantly — not just on scheduled calls. They prospect upsell and new business in parallel.

Habit 2

Reports drive priority

Product Adoption Report, Retail Dealer Performance, Power BI, SEM Rush, SpyFu, Control Center. The data tells you who to call.

Habit 3

Lead with value

Walk in with a specific observation about their store, not a generic ask. "Are you interested in" never works.

Habit 4

Pre-call planning is the work

Audit the website, pull the SEO report, know what they don't have. The call is the result of the prep.

"I'm always coming in with something of value. I'm not calling, 'Are you interested in learning about this?' I'm saying, 'Hey, I noticed you have 67 cars that have sat on your lot for 90 days. What's your plan to get those off your lot?'"

Laura Charbonneau · Vermont

Module 2 · Discovery

Read the room before you pitch

A six-store group is not six single-store conversations. Decision-maker, leverage, and proof points all change.

Single rooftop

  • Decision-maker is usually in front of you
  • Lead with operational pain — pace of sales, lot age, lead handling
  • Focus on the one product that solves the loudest problem
  • Faster cycle, but less leverage on price

Group / multi-rooftop

  • Ask early — do stores decide individually or as a group?
  • Lead with consistency, reporting, ecosystem story
  • Bring in MSS — they have pricing levers L1 doesn't
  • Longer cycle, much bigger deal size

"He told me in discovery that he would sooner be dead than use Dealer.com. I made a mental note: don't pitch your product. By the end of the 40-minute conversation he said, 'You know what — go ahead and put Dealer.com on that quote.' Just relationship. Just qualifying questions instead of pitching."

Tyler Pickering · Missouri

Module 3 · Demo & objections

PC drives the demo. You drive the relationship.

Almost every top rep uses the same model — and the 15-minute pre-call brief is non-negotiable.

Before the demo

  • 15-minute call with the PC to share discovery
  • Hand over what the dealer has said — not just what the data shows
  • Align on the one or two products that matter most
  • Decide who handles which objection

During the demo

  • PC shows the product, you read the room
  • Be in-store if you can — face-to-face wins momentum
  • Stay 10–15 min after the demo to discuss
  • Don't end without locking the next step

"I have a 15-minute call with the PC before the demo so I can share what I gathered in my own discovery. I come from the relationship side — I know things the dealer has said versus what we can see in their performance."

Logan Hardwick · Washington

"Having me in store while the demo is going has been our biggest success. They can ask me questions during. The biggest thing is conversations before and conversations after — instead of everyone hanging up and being gone."

Chad Moreland · Kansas

Module 4 · Collaboration

The five partnerships top reps maintain

Every interview surfaced the same point: top reps invest in internal relationships before they need them.

Product Consultants

The technical engine

The 15-min pre-call is non-negotiable. Hand them context, not just a calendar invite.

Performance Mgrs

Bi-weekly cadence

Two-way street. Ask how you can help them save accounts before asking for referrals.

MSS team

The leverage you don't have

They have pricing, bundling, rebates that L1 doesn't. Bring them in early.

Cross-BU

vAuto, Autotrader, Manheim

Referrals both ways. Their reps are in markets you're not.

Leadership

The title is the unlock

If a deal stalls a week, escalate. A director can sweeten the pot.

"I reached out to Sylvia, the performance manager. She said, 'I have a great relationship with Casey — I talk to him a couple times a week.' She gave me word tracks, offered to call him for me. We walked in saying, 'Sylvia thinks Vin Solutions would be a great fit.' Set the meeting that day."

Module 4 · Product Consultants

PCs are partners, not pinch hitters

The top reps don't just hand off a calendar invite. They feed PCs context, set them up to win, and stay in the room to close.

Before the demo

  • Lock the PC's calendar — confirm via chat, don't assume
  • 15-minute prep call to share discovery and stakeholders
  • Drop notes in SFX: who, their job, current products, what they've said
  • Send the brief 1–2 hours before, not a week before

During & after

  • PC drives the technical demo — you read the room and steer
  • You stay in to close — don't let the dealer hang up after
  • You hear the pushbacks live — that's what fuels follow-up
  • Recap email same day, with proposal embedded

"I send the PC: who we're talking to, their job, the products they have, what they've been saying. Just a paragraph. I send it 1–2 hours before — not a week before — so it's fresh."

Scott McCulloch · Kansas

"PCs are kind of the tech guy — there's less pressure. They say, 'I'm just here to show you.' It becomes show and tell, not show and sell."

Joe Grubb · Kentucky

Module 4 · Closing & follow-up

When the dealer goes quiet

There's no magic close. Top reps have a system, and they execute it the same way every time.

1
Send your outside partner into the store

The most repeated answer across the interviews. Face-to-face beats every email.

Ben Aikins, Ryan Rowley, Logan Hardwick
2
Same-day recap with proposal embedded

Use Copilot to transcribe the Teams call. Recap email goes out same day with pricing in it.

Logan Hardwick · Washington
3
48-hour rule, then tag-team

If no response in 48 hours, inside and outside both reach out — one a day each.

Logan Hardwick · Washington
4
Lock the slot on the call

"Don't say 'I'll follow up.' Ask: 'When's the best day and time for me to call you to follow up?' Lock the slot."

Tyler Pickering · Missouri
5
The most underrated closing technique

"Stop talking and listen. Most of the time it's silent. The better you can hold off, the more likely you have them in a position to buy."

Module 5 · Mindset

Sales is never as high or as low as it seems

Every top rep had a version of this advice. Consistency comes from accepting the ebb — and not changing what works.

"
If I go three days without selling, I'm like, 'I suck at my job.' But sales is never as high or as low as it may seem. You're going to have killer months, okay months, and bad months. Just ride the wave and stay consistent.
